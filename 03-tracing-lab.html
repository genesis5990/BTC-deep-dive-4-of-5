Tracing Lab: Sample Dataset "RANSOM-7"

Fabricated data: every TXID, address, and timestamp below is invented for training. Real TXIDs are 64 hex characters; we truncate for readability and you record the full-length form in your worksheet. Do not run these against a live explorer. A printable version of this exercise is on page 8.

Scenario briefing (read aloud)

A local hospital reports a ransomware incident. The note demands 2.5 BTC. Their insurer's incident handler captured the payment transaction hash from their wallet. Your task: trace where the money goes, and identify the point where legal process could yield a name.

Known starting point: the hospital's outgoing payment tx T1. Everything else you must discover from the chain.

Guided trace — work it in pairs (15 min)

Step 1 · Anchor. Confirm T1: which output is the ransom, which is the hospital's change? (2.5000 round → R1 ransom; 0.0117 same-type fresh → change.)
Step 2 · First hop. T2 spends R1's entire UTXO 49 minutes later. One output is H1a (2.4994); the other is an OP_RETURN carrying 0 BTC. Fee = 2.5000 − 2.4994 = 0.0006 BTC. Whoever signed T2 controls R1 → R1 and H1a are the same entity. Record this inference and its basis (key control, not guesswork).
Step 3 · The trap row (T3). T3 claims to spend R1's UTXO — impossible; it's already spent in T2. On a real explorer this row simply wouldn't exist. Teaching point: UTXOs are spent exactly once. If your tool ever appears to show two spends, verify before you conclude anything.
Step 4 · The split. T4 sends ~50/50 to two fresh addresses H2a/H2b, created and signed in one transaction → same controller. Your working cluster: {R1, H1a, H2a, H2b}.
Step 5 · Cash-out. T5 and T6 both land at bc1q…ex within 19 minutes. That address receives from 40+ unrelated sources → classic service deposit address (Heuristic 3). T7 is the service's internal consolidation sweep. End of the on-chain trail.
Step 6 · Legal handoff. The blockchain can't name the person — but the service can. Preserve T1–T7 records, timestamps, amounts, and your heuristic notes (see the hashing box on page 5). Legal-process target: the platform's KYC records for deposit address bc1q…ex, withdrawal history tied to the T7 consolidation, and session/IP logs. That is how a trace becomes a suspect.
Timing insight worth noting in your report: victim → service in ~7 hours, with short dwell times at each hop (49 min, ~2.5 h, 19 min). Fast, scripted movement is a hallmark of a payout pipeline, not a personal wallet.
